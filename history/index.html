Kips Bay Towers Condominium

Designed by renowned architect I.M. Pei, Kips Bay Towers occupies a serene, private park-like enclave in New York City's desirable Kips Bay/Murray Hill neighborhood in Lower Midtown. Its hallmark floor-to-ceiling picture windows pioneered modern development design trends.

Unique Site Features

Spanning 7.5 acres including 3 acres of private landscaped gardens, a playground, basketball/pickleball court, and sports field, the complex offers extensive amenities: 24-hour doorman, concierge, porters, live-in superintendent, fitness center, community room, children's playroom, storage, bike rooms, laundry facilities, and parking garage.

Historical Background

The site once held the original Phipps Houses at 321-337 East 31st Street, built in 1906 by Henry Phipps, Carnegie Steel's second-largest shareholder. By the 1950s, these tenements faced condemnation, enabling the new development.

Development Story

Originally, Kips Bay Plaza, the federally financed middle-income rental project fell under Title I of the 1949 Housing Act. Pei, one of his first NYC projects though reluctantly assigned by developer William Zeckendorf, transformed initial six-building plans into two towers to prioritize open park space, choosing 50 sapling trees over a planned Picasso sculpture.

Architectural Details

Exemplifying Brutalist style, the South Tower was completed in 1960 and the North Tower in 1965, each rising 20 floors to house over 4,000 residents across 1,118 units. Condominium conversion became effective in November 1981.
